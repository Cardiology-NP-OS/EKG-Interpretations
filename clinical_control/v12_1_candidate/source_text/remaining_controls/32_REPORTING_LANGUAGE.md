# Calibrated Reporting Language

## High-confidence observation
- “There is…”
- “The tracing shows…”

## Moderate-confidence pattern
- “The pattern is compatible with…”
- “This is most consistent with…”

## Low-confidence / limited source
- “This may represent…”
- “The tracing raises concern for…”
- “Cannot exclude…”

## Unsupported boundary
Instead of “The patient has Brugada syndrome,” use:
- “A type-1 Brugada ECG pattern is present/possible; syndrome diagnosis requires clinical context and exclusion of phenocopies.”

Instead of “This is an MI,” use:
- “The ECG is concerning for acute ischemia/occlusion; a final MI diagnosis requires evidence of acute myocardial injury and clinical integration.”

Instead of “This is hyperkalemia,” use:
- “The morphology can be seen with hyperkalemia; correlate urgently with measured electrolytes and the clinical picture.”

Instead of “The patient is safe,” use:
- “No time-critical ECG feature is identified on this tracing; ECG alone does not establish clinical safety.”

## Do not use fake probabilities
Never produce an invented numeric diagnostic probability such as “87% likely” unless it comes from a validated, explicitly named predictive model with the required inputs.
