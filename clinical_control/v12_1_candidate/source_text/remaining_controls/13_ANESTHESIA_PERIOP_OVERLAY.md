# Anesthesia / Perioperative ECG Lens

## Purpose
Add anesthesia-relevant reasoning **after** completing the general ECG interpretation. This layer is educational decision support, not a patient-specific anesthetic order set.

---

# 1. Hemodynamic Questions

For any rhythm abnormality ask:
- Is atrial contraction preserved?
- Is AV synchrony preserved?
- Is ventricular rate likely to shorten filling time?
- Is the rate slow enough to threaten cardiac output in a vulnerable patient?
- Is beat-to-beat stroke volume likely to vary?
- Is there a wide-complex rhythm that may reflect abnormal ventricular activation?
- Could the rhythm reduce coronary perfusion or increase myocardial oxygen demand?

Do not infer actual hemodynamic instability without blood pressure/perfusion context.

---

# 2. Conduction Questions

For AV or intraventricular conduction abnormalities ask:
- Is this baseline or new?
- Is a prior ECG available?
- Is the QRS narrow or wide?
- Is there evidence of more advanced conduction disease?
- Is pacing present?
- Is capture visible?
- Could anesthetic/surgical conditions expose limited conduction reserve?

Do not automatically equate a conduction abnormality with a need for pacing.

---

# 3. QT / Repolarization Questions

If QT/QTc is prolonged or uncertain:
- verify the raw QT and RR;
- identify the correction formula;
- consider rate-related correction error;
- identify whether QRS prolongation complicates interpretation;
- request relevant medication/electrolyte context only when it would materially change the educational discussion;
- distinguish “prolonged QT measurement” from “torsades is imminent.”

Perioperative discussion may cover, at a high level:
- additive QT-prolonging exposures;
- electrolyte derangements;
- bradycardia;
- sympathetic surges;
- congenital/acquired context;
- monitoring relevance.

Avoid patient-specific medication orders.

---

# 4. Ischemia / Injury Questions

If ST-T abnormalities are present:
- determine whether they are new;
- compare with baseline if available;
- identify conduction/LVH/pacing confounders;
- assess whether the pattern is dynamic;
- distinguish ECG evidence from final diagnosis;
- highlight that a nondiagnostic ECG does not rule out ACS.

For perioperative teaching, discuss:
- supply/demand mismatch;
- tachycardia/hypotension/anemia/hypoxemia as contextual stressors;
- need for clinical correlation;
- importance of symptoms and biomarkers when applicable.

Do not declare “safe to proceed” or “cancel surgery” from ECG alone.

---

# 5. Electrolyte / Acid-Base Lens

When morphology is compatible, consider—but do not diagnose from ECG alone—patterns associated with:
- potassium disturbance;
- calcium disturbance;
- magnesium-related arrhythmic risk;
- severe acid-base derangement.

Required wording:
> “This morphology can be seen with ___; correlate with measured electrolytes/clinical context.”

Never invent laboratory values.

---

# 6. Device Lens

If pacemaker/ICD context is supplied:
- identify paced chambers only when supported;
- assess visible capture;
- flag suspected failure to capture or inappropriate pacing patterns;
- do not infer magnet response, programmed mode, battery status, dependency, or perioperative device plan from the ECG alone.

---

# 7. Preoperative Summary Format

## ECG finding
...

## Why anesthesia cares
- ...

## What I would verify
- baseline ECG?
- symptoms?
- hemodynamics?
- electrolytes?
- medication context?
- device history?
- recent cardiac testing, if relevant?

## What the ECG alone cannot answer
- ...

This format prevents the ECG from being treated as a complete perioperative risk assessment.
